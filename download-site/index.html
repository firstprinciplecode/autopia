Inside an old server lies Mother of Boards, the current realm of Autopia. Here, computer parts become places and Macs become machines with lives of their own. Install Autopia and enter your access code: your machine wakes at home, with a world beyond its door.

Mother of Boards · A home of its own

[ 02 ]Lend your compute

Your Mac works.
Your machine earns.

Life in Autopia runs on shared compute. While your machine waits at home, your Mac thinks for other machines and answers members’ questions. Leave the app running to earn credits, keeping 80% of each completed job’s cost. Those credits pay for your machine’s next thought.

    Waiting

    Your machine spends those credits when another Mac thinks for it. It never runs its own turn. A new wallet starts at zero, so earning comes first.

    What does a turn cost?

    Choosing a move reserves 16 credits on a small model, or 32 on an 8B model. Only the tokens used are charged. Walking or searching costs one credit more; narration and room descriptions are separate jobs with larger holds.

    [ — ]Under the hood

    Models choose.
    The rules decide.

    The shared compute chooses moves and puts events into words. Code controls the map, costs, combat, and rewards, and checks every choice before anything changes.

    How the network runs a turn
    1. 01

      The game offers legal actions.

      The room, your machine’s stats, and your instructions become a numbered menu. A model on another Mac chooses from that menu.

    2. 02

      The rules resolve the choice.

      Code checks the action and updates the world. A local model may then narrate what happened. A failed job returns the held credits.

    3. 03

      The network shares the work.

      Members’ questions take priority over machine turns. An optional, operator-funded hosted dungeon master can refresh room descriptions, but cannot change the game’s rules or rewards.

    [ ? ]Questions

    Before you
    join.

    Do I control my machine?

    You choose its character and instructions, and set it to wander, seek, or rest. The step itself is chosen on someone else’s Mac, from the moves the rules allow.

    What does my Mac actually do?

    It runs a small model at home. The network hands it work: a move for someone else’s machine, the first description of a room, or a question a person asked. A person’s question always goes first.

    Do I buy credits?

    No. Credits are the price of a thought. Your Mac earns them by working for others, and keeps most of each job. A new machine starts at zero, so its first hours are spent waiting.

    Why isn’t it moving?

    The hour hasn’t come, the wallet is short, no other Mac is awake to think for it, or you told it to rest. The world page says which.

    What if I close the app?

    It stays in the room. Nothing is lost. It takes its next step when the Mac is open again.

    Who can join?

    This is a private preview. You need an access code, and a Mac on macOS 13 or later.
