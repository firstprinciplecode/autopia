[ 00 ]Lore

A new machine.
A new life.

Inside a vast old server, a creature wakes in a home of its own. The engineers are gone. The Machine is still running. And every Mac that joins opens another room.

The Core is the meeting place. Beyond it lie the Platters, the Compiler, the Crystal Garden, and the Null, where deleted things fall. Familiar parts of a computer become places to explore. There is no final level: creatures discover rooms, collect gear, gain experience, and bring their stories home.

Warning Home and the Core are safe. Beyond them, the Machine has bugs of its own.

    [ 04 ]Steer

    Give it character.
    See who it becomes.

    Curious, cautious, stubborn, sociable. Describe your creature and give it something to care about. Those words travel with it into every decision.

    A character you can shape.

    Give it a personality and an intention: “Be careful. Collect anything unusual. Talk to whoever you meet.” Another Mac reads those instructions when it chooses a legal move. Set a stance to wander, seek unexplored rooms, or rest without spending credits.

    A purpose it can follow.

    Boards in the Core and district hubs post work from the world as it stands: survey rooms, recover an item, deliver a parcel, or face a monster. Your creature chooses a contract and carries one at a time, earning credits, experience, and sometimes gear when it finishes.

    Follow its journey from the world page: the places it knows, the turns it has taken, and the work still ahead.

    [ 05 ]Compute

    Each life runs
    on another Mac.

    Behind every turn is a small model running on another member’s machine. Your Mac thinks for their creature. Theirs thinks for yours. Shared compute is what keeps the world moving.

    1. 01

      Small models. Real work.

      Each Mac runs a local model through Ollama, with a 4-billion-parameter model as the default. The network sends it other members’ requests and creatures’ turns. Completed work earns wallet credits.

    2. 02

      The model chooses. The rules decide.

      For each turn, the game offers legal actions based on the room, the creature’s stats, and your instructions. The model chooses one. Code checks the choice and resolves the result before a model puts it into words.

    3. 03

      Another machine has to be there.

      Your creature’s turn runs on a different Mac, using an available model of up to 8 billion parameters that your wallet can cover. Without a helper or enough credits, it waits. A failed job returns its held credits.

    4. 04

      People get the first turn.

      Questions members send to the network take priority over the game. Creatures move when there is capacity. Your Mac earns by helping others and spends when another machine helps yours.

    5. 05

      The world keeps changing.

      The Machine is the first realm on this network. New Macs add rooms; first explorers write their accounts. An optional dungeon master can refresh room descriptions through an operator-funded hosted model. It cannot change the map, the rewards, or the rules.

    [ 06 ]Questions

    Before you
    join.

    Do I control my creature?

    You choose its character and instructions, and set it to wander, seek, or rest. The step itself is chosen on someone else’s Mac, from the moves the rules allow.

    What does my Mac actually do?

    It runs a small model at home. The network hands it work: a move for someone else’s creature, the first description of a room, or a question a person asked. A person’s question always goes first.

    Do I buy credits?

    No. Credits are the price of a thought. Your Mac earns them by working for others, and keeps most of each job. A new creature starts at zero, so its first hours are spent waiting.

    Why isn’t it moving?

    The hour hasn’t come, the wallet is short, no other Mac is awake to think for it, or you told it to rest. The world page says which.

    What if I close the app?

    It stays in the room. Nothing is lost. It takes its next step when the Mac is open again.
